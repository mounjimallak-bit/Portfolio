Axiora Data

A validated dataset and a commune-level analysis of poverty and population in the Souss-Massa region of Morocco, built from 93 tables published by the High Commission for Planning (HCP).

Open the live app View source

Summary

Official statistics for the six territories of Souss-Massa are published as PDF tables that are hard to compare across years and places. This project transcribed 93 of these tables, checked them automatically, and used them to answer descriptive questions about population, housing, finance and poverty. The main analysis concerns the change in commune poverty rates between 2004 and 2014.

Research question

Did poverty decline uniformly across the communes of Souss-Massa between 2004 and 2014, and where did it decline least?

The question is descriptive. It asks how the distribution of commune poverty rates changed and how the change differed between territories. It does not test a causal hypothesis.

Data and method

Source. The rates come from the HCP communal poverty evolution sheets for 2004, 2007 and 2014, which report the poverty rate for each commune of the six territories. Subtotal rows (urban, rural and total for each territory) are excluded, leaving 182 communes.

Sample. The analysis uses the 179 communes that have a rate in all three years. Three urban centre units (AC) of Chtouka-Aït Baha have no rate in the 2004 or 2014 sheets and are excluded. Because the sample is a complete-case sample, the results describe these 179 communes only.

Notation. Let xi, yi and zi be the poverty rates (in percent) of commune i in 2004, 2007 and 2014, for the 179 communes of the sample. The change for commune i is di = zi - xi, in percentage points.

Descriptive statistics. Distributions are summarised by the median, the 25th and 75th percentiles, and the interquartile range IQR = Q3 - Q1. Each commune counts once, regardless of its population. Population weights are not used because the commune population series needed to compute them is not part of the dataset.

Inference. Three tests are reported. (i) A two-sided exact sign test of the hypothesis that a commune is equally likely to decline or increase, applied to the signs of di. (ii) A two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the paired values (xi, zi), which does not assume normality of the differences. (iii) A 95% confidence interval for the median change, obtained by the percentile bootstrap with 10,000 resamples of the 179 communes and a fixed random seed (42).

Validation. Before analysis, every table passes an automated check of column lengths, totals and territory names. The check reports no errors on the current dataset (see Data and validation below).

Results

The median commune poverty rate fell from 23.0% to 10.2%, a decrease of 12.8 percentage points, or 55.5% of the 2004 median. The decline was not evenly spread over the period. The median fell by 9.0 points between 2004 and 2007 and by 3.8 points between 2007 and 2014, so most of the decrease occurred in the first three years.

The spread between communes also narrowed. The interquartile range was 14.3 points in 2004 (17.2% to 31.5%) and 9.7 points in 2014 (5.8% to 15.5%). The gap between the lowest and highest communes was therefore smaller in 2014.

Change by commune and statistical tests

171 of the 179 communes (95.5%) recorded a lower rate in 2014 than in 2004. The remaining eight recorded an increase, with a largest increase of 4.2 points. The largest decline was 40.4 points. The median change is −12.5 points, with a 95% bootstrap confidence interval of −14.2 to −11.2 points.

The p-values indicate that the decline is not an artefact of a few large changes: the sign test depends only on the direction of change, and the Wilcoxon test depends on its ranks. Neither test says anything about the size of the effect, which is given by the median change and its interval.

Tata shows the smallest decline. Its median fell from 21.2% to 16.6%, the smallest fall of any territory, and its 2014 median is the highest of the six. Taroudant has the highest 2004 median, at 28.1%, and fell to 11.7% by 2014. Inezgane-Aït Melloul and Agadir Ida Outanane have the lowest 2014 medians.

Discussion

The results show a large and widespread decline in commune poverty rates between 2004 and 2014, with most of the decline concentrated before 2007. They also show that the decline was uneven: Tata, in particular, retained higher rates than the other territories in 2014. The data show where the declines occurred and how large they were; they do not show why. Explaining the differences would require data on local investment, employment and infrastructure, which are outside the dataset.

Two features of the analysis limit how far the results can be generalised. First, the statistics are unweighted, so a small commune and a large commune count equally; a population-weighted measure could give a different picture. Second, the sample excludes three urban centre units for which no rate is published in two of the three years. The three years are also the only years for which the poverty sheets were available in the dataset.

Population and sectors

Population grew by 15.2% between the RGPH 2004 and RGPH 2014 censuses, from 2,324,142 to 2,676,847. Growth was concentrated in Inezgane-Aït Melloul (+29.0%) and Agadir Ida Outanane (+23.1%), while Tiznit (−4.5%) and Tata (−3.1%) declined.

Regional bank credit equals 59.9% of deposits (29.70 billion DH of credit against 49.56 billion DH of deposits). Agadir Ida Outanane has the highest ratio, at 80.2%.

Data and validation

The 93 tables are stored as aligned columns and pass an automated check before they are used. The check verifies that columns have equal length, that urban plus rural equals total and men plus women equals total, that the regional row equals the sum of the provinces within 1%, that a rate of exactly zero is not treated as a missing value, and that every territory name matches a polygon of the official HCP boundary files. On the current dataset, the check reports no errors and no warnings. It also identified a misaligned row in a 2017 housing table, which was corrected against the yearbook.

Limitations

  • Chtouka-Aït Baha, urban population in 2004. The HCP sheets do not give this figure. The value 39,694 is an inference, chosen so that the province figures sum to the published regional total. It affects the 2004 urban share of the region.
  • Unweighted statistics. Each commune counts once. A population-weighted analysis would require commune population series that are not part of the dataset.
  • Labour tables for 2017 to 2020. These come from the regional results of the ENE survey and were not checked cell by cell against a single published table. They are not used for any result on this page. Most other numeric tables were cross-checked value by value against their source.
  • Projections. The application also extrapolates trends with linear regression. These extrapolations have no held-out evaluation and are not used here.
  • Scope. The analysis covers Souss-Massa only and is descriptive. Vulnerability rates are in the dataset but are not analysed here.

Stack and access

Python, pandas, NumPy, Plotly, GeoPandas, Streamlit. The live application and the source code, including the tables and the validation script, are public.
